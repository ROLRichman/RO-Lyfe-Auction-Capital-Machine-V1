Overview

The RO’Lyfe Tax Yield & County Intelligence Engine™ is the county-research and tax-investment intelligence layer of the RO’Lyfe Auction Capital Machine.

Its purpose is to organize the information that investors, brokers, lenders, and acquisition teams need before pursuing tax-defaulted real estate, tax lien certificates, tax deeds, redeemable deeds, foreclosure-related assets, repository properties, and other county-controlled opportunities.

The engine recognizes a critical fact:

Every county can operate differently.

States may establish the legal framework, but counties and local agencies can differ in terminology, auction platforms, registration requirements, bidding methods, payment deadlines, redemption procedures, post-sale disposition, OTC availability, documentation, and research resources.

The engine therefore does not treat a generic state-level chart as sufficient due diligence.

It creates a structured County Intelligence Profile.

The workflow is:

Find the County → Research the Process → Verify the Official Sources → Understand the Asset → Analyze the Risk → Underwrite the Opportunity → Decide

The engine is designed to answer:

- What happens when property taxes become delinquent?
- What is the county's timeline?
- What is sold: a lien, certificate, deed, or other interest?
- What is the redemption process?
- How is interest or penalty calculated?
- How does the auction work?
- What bidding method is used?
- What deposit is required?
- What happens to unsuccessful bidders?
- What happens when a property does not sell?
- Is there an OTC program?
- Is there a repository or county-held inventory?
- How does a tax certificate progress toward a tax deed?
- What records should be reviewed?
- Which official offices control each step?
- What documents should be collected before bidding?
- What risks should stop the transaction?

Asset Classification

The engine separates several fundamentally different opportunity types.

Tax Lien

The investor acquires a tax-related lien or certificate rather than automatically acquiring the property.

The owner may retain ownership and may redeem the lien according to the applicable jurisdiction's rules.

Tax Deed

The investor bids on property being sold through a tax-deed process.

The acquisition, title, redemption, notice, and post-sale rules must be verified for the applicable jurisdiction.

Redeemable Deed

A jurisdiction-specific structure in which the purchaser's interest remains subject to statutory redemption or related procedures.

Foreclosure

A judicial or statutory process that can affect ownership and lien priority.

REO

Property already controlled by a lender, institution, government entity, or other seller following foreclosure or another disposition process.

Repository / County-Held / Post-Sale Inventory

Properties that remain unsold after a tax sale may enter a jurisdiction-specific post-sale status.

The engine does not automatically classify every unsold property as "OTC."

County Research Call Workflow

The engine converts county research into a structured interview.

Basic Tax Default Process

Ask:

1. When does a property become delinquent?
2. What happens after delinquency?
3. What is the normal timeline?
4. Can the timeline vary?
5. Where is the official process documented?
6. Is there a county handout or webpage explaining the process?
7. Which office handles each stage?

Redemption

Ask:

1. Is there a redemption period?
2. Who can redeem?
3. When does the redemption period begin?
4. When does it end?
5. What interest applies?
6. What penalties apply?
7. Are costs added?
8. Is interest calculated monthly, annually, or another way?
9. What happens after redemption?

Auction

Ask:

1. How are tax liens or properties sold?
2. Is the sale online?
3. Is it in person?
4. Which auction platform is used?
5. Is registration required?
6. Is a deposit required?
7. What is the minimum bid?
8. What is the bidding increment?
9. Is bidding based on price?
10. Is interest bid down?
11. Is ownership bid down?
12. Is a premium added?
13. Is sealed bidding used?
14. When is payment due?
15. What happens when the winning bidder fails to pay?
16. What happens to a non-winning bidder's deposit?

OTC / Post-Sale Inventory

Ask:

1. What happens to liens or properties that do not sell?
2. Does the county retain them?
3. Can the public purchase them later?
4. Is there an OTC program?
5. When does the OTC inventory become available?
6. Where is the list published?
7. How is the purchase price calculated?
8. Is the purchase first-come, first-served?
9. Is registration or an application required?
10. Are there additional restrictions?

Research Hierarchy

The engine should prioritize information in this order:

Tier 1 — Official Government Source

State statutes, county tax collector, county tax claim bureau, clerk of court, recorder, assessor/property appraiser, official auction portal, official sale rules.

Tier 2 — Official Auction Platform

Auction terms, bidder instructions, deposit requirements, payment deadlines, sale status, property documents, and transaction rules.

Tier 3 — Property / Market Research

Property records, GIS, recorded documents, comparable sales, market data, maps, condition information, occupancy information.

Tier 4 — Secondary Research

Industry guides, educational resources, investor references, and state-by-state summaries.

Secondary research can help identify what to investigate, but it should not replace current county rules.
